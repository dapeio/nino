## Hooks in `Nino.css`

A design layer - the delivered `assets/theme.css`, the catalogue's Design feature, a project's own `assets/style.css` - changes the look by setting custom properties in `:root`, after `Nino.css` in the bundle. These hold the faces, the sizes and the numbers that scale them. Every default is what the rules had before the property existed, so a page that sets none of them looks as it did. A few cases differ, and each is named where it is described: a subtitle class, or `.nino-code`, on an element the browser makes bold - an `h1` to `h6`, or anything inside a `strong` - takes the weight of its role, `normal`, where it was bold; the `--quiet` and `--loud` variants have other sizes; a bare `code` inside an element that states its size in rem is smaller than it was, except in the kernel's own text classes (below); and a card that stood out of its section by itself has to ask for it.

| Property | Default | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `--fontfamily-title`, `-subtitle`, `-text`, `-button`, `-code` | the system stack; `-button` is `var(--fontfamily-text)` | the family of the five font roles |
| `--font-<role>-weight` | `bold` for `title` and `button`, `normal` for the rest | the weight of a role |
| `--font-<role>-transform` | `none` | the `text-transform` of a role |
| `--font-<role>-size` | `title` `var(--text-4)`, `subtitle` `var(--text-2)`, `text` `1rem`, `button` `var(--fontsize-btn)`, `code` `.875rem` | the size of a role |
| `--hero-title-scale`, `--hero-text-scale` | `1.2` and `1.2`; from 768px `1.25` and `10 / 9` | the size of `.nino-hero-title` and `.nino-hero-subtitle`: the size of the title and of the subtitle role times the number |
| `--article-title-scale` | `5 / 6`; from 768px `25 / 32` | the size of `.nino-article-title`: the size of the title role times the number |
| `--scale-quiet`, `--scale-loud` | `.85` and `1.2` | the `--quiet` and the `--loud` variants of a section, an article and a hero: the size of the class they vary - the title, subtitle or text role, and for an article title and a hero the scale of that class on top - times the number |
| `--grid-gutter` | `var(--space-2)`; from 768px `var(--space-3)` | the horizontal padding of `.nino-grid-row`, the space between the viewport and a row |
| `--shadow-card` | `none` | the `box-shadow` of `.nino-article` and `.nino-pricing-item` |
| `--color-ground`, `--color-ink` | `var(--color-section-default-bg)` and `var(--color-text)` | the surface a thing is on and the ink written on it: a section, a column and a card that paint a ground set them for everything in them |
| `--color-border-<ground>` | `var(--color-border)`, for `default`, `alt`, `tint`, `dark`, `black`, `primary` and `brand-alt` | the border of a ground: inside a section, a column or a card of that ground `--color-border` is this one, so a design layer can give each ground a border of its own. The delivered `theme.css` sets none of them |
| `--counter-grid-<part>`, `--counter-grid-next-<part>`, `--counter-card-<part>` | the page's: a counter column becomes `alt`, a counter card in it `default`, a counter card on the page `alt`; `<part>` is `bg`, `text`, `title`, `accent`, `focus` and `border` | the counter ground, see below. The framework sets these; a design layer sets the sources they are taken from and never one of them |
| `--color-error`, `--color-success`, `--color-warning` and the `-text` of each | `#f44336`, `#4caf50` and `#b45309`; the ink is `var(--color-primary-text)` | the surface of a status and the ink solved for it: `.nino-alert--error`, `--success` and `--warning`, `.nino-badge--success` and `--error`, the message of a form and the toast; the check mark of `.nino-list--check` is written in `--color-success`. Every rule reads the role, none writes the colour |

- **The roles.** Title, subtitle, text, button and code. The title role is read by `.nino-section-title`, `.nino-article-title` and `.nino-hero-title`, and by every `h1` to `h6` for its weight and its transform alone: a heading without a class keeps the face of the text and the size the browser gives it. The subtitle role is read by the three subtitles, the text role by the page (size, weight and transform), the button role by `.nino-btn` and its `--small` and `--big` forms (and by the filter button, for the size) and the code role by `.nino-code`. A `code`, `kbd`, `samp` or `pre` with no class of its own belongs to no role: it takes `.8125em` of the size around it - 13 of 16, the smaller size the browser gives it - and one inside another of the four is not made smaller twice. That smaller size is a quirk of the browser's, and it holds only where the size around the code comes from keywords: inside an element that states its size in rem the browser gave it the size of that element. The kernel's own sized classes for text, titles, prices, counts and labels keep that - in `.nino-section-text` and `.nino-article-descr`, in the title and the subtitle of a section, of an article and of a hero, in the title and the price of the pricing table, each with its `--quiet` and `--loud`, and in `.nino-article-price`, `.nino-stat-counter`, `.nino-form-message`, `.nino-btn` and `.nino-filter-btn`, such a code is `1em`, the size of its text - and in any other element that states its size in rem - a class of a project's own, a menu link of the kernel - it is `.8125em` of it, smaller than it was.
- **The scale.** `--text-1` is `--font-text-size`; `--text-2` to `--text-6` are `--font-text-size` times `1.125`, `1.25`, `1.5`, `1.8` and `2.5`, and from 768px `--text-4` to `--text-6` times `1.6`, `2` and `3`. **The delivered `assets/theme.css` assigns `--text-1` to `--text-6` itself**, from its own raster, and what it assigns wins over `Nino.css`: on a project made from it the scale follows `--font-text-size` once those six lines are gone.
- **The hero, the article title and the variants.** The leading of a hero moves with its size. An article title is five sixths of the title role (`25 / 32` from 768px), which is the `--text-3` it stood on where the other titles stand on `--text-4`. The two factors that fall from 768px (`--article-title-scale`, `5 / 6` to `25 / 32`, and `--hero-text-scale`, `1.2` to `10 / 9`) give back the sizes the rules had before there were roles only while the size of the role they multiply is a step of the scale: a design layer that fixes `--font-title-size` or `--font-subtitle-size` in rem sets these two factors as well, or the article title and the hero subtitle get smaller in rem from 768px. A `--quiet` or `--loud` variant is the size of the class it varies - `--font-title-size`, `--font-subtitle-size` or `--font-text-size`, and for an article title and a hero times `--article-title-scale`, `--hero-title-scale` or `--hero-text-scale` - times `--scale-quiet` or `--scale-loud`, and keeps the ratio of its leading and its opacity. So a design layer that makes the title role larger takes the quiet and the loud title with it, and two numbers move every variant. The variants of the pricing table are not part of this and stand on the scale.
- **Where to set them.** On `:root`. The size of a role is resolved there - `--font-title-size` is the `--text-4` of the root - so a `--text-n` or a `--fontsize-btn` that a section or a column sets for itself does not reach the base title, subtitle and button classes in it, nor their `--quiet` and `--loud` variants, which are the size of their role times a number. To size those inside one section, set the `--font-<role>-size` there. The text classes `.nino-section-text` and `.nino-article-descr` stand on the scale and read the step themselves, so they do follow a `--text-n` that a section or a column sets for itself; their variants read the size of the text role. A hero title or subtitle that carries its base class next to the variant follows it below 768px only: from there the base class states its size again.
- **A card.** `.nino-article` (and with it a `.nino-bento-item` that carries it, as the catalogue draws them) and `.nino-pricing-item` read `--shadow-card`. A `.nino-article--borderless` has none, and the middle card of `.nino-pricing-row--feature-middle` and the toast keep the shadows they have.
- **Grounds.** A section, a column and a card paint a ground with the same words - `.nino-section--<x>`, `.nino-grid--<x>` and `.nino-article--<x>`, with `<x>` one of `alt`, `tint`, `dark`, `black`, `primary` and `brand-alt` - and a column and a card may also ask for the counter ground, below. A ground is a surface, the ink written on it and the roles solved against the pair. It sets `--color-ground` and `--color-ink`, and `--color-title`, `--color-subtitle`, `--color-accent`, `--color-focus` and `--color-border` for everything in it, and paints itself with the first two. A title, a link, a button or a ring never has to know which ground it was dropped onto: a rule that reads a role is right on every ground, and a rule or a design layer that wants the pair itself - to mix a hover out of it, to draw a hairline on it - reads `--color-ground` and `--color-ink`. The title and the subtitle of a hero read `--color-title` and `--color-subtitle`, the outline button `--color-accent` for its text and for its border, and the filter button and the arrow of a hero take the ink the ground paints; no rule of a section stands above them, so inside a column or a card of another ground they are in the colours of that column or card, and the outline button of a dark ground is written in the accent of that ground - the link colour a design layer solves for it, and without one the ink of the ground. What puts the page ground back inside a section - an alert, a modal, the open burger menu, the toast, the striped rows of a table, the open language picker, a dimmed cover or picture - sets the pair it paints, and the status alerts, toasts, badges and form messages, which paint a surface of their own, the pair of their status. A column that paints a ground, or the counter ground, keeps its content off the colour (`padding: var(--space-2)`) and is rounded as a card is (`var(--radius)`); a card of any ground has the border and the padding the alt card had. A `.nino-article--borderless` takes the border and the shadow away and keeps the ground a card names; only a plain card without a border is transparent. **Nothing compares the ground of a thing with the ground it sits on**: a `.nino-article--alt` in an alt section is alt on alt, because its author asked for it.
- **The counter ground.** A column or a card that should stand out of what it sits on says so by name, `.nino-grid--counter` or `.nino-article--counter`, and follows the section when it changes its colour. The pairs are fixed:

  | The ground | Its counter |
  |---|---|
  | default (the page) | `alt` |
  | `alt` | default |
  | `tint` | default - a card of the alt surface on a wash of the page reads worse than the page's own |
  | `dark` | `black` |
  | `black` | `dark` |
  | `primary` | `brand-alt` |
  | `brand-alt` | `primary` |

  Nothing is told which ground it sits on: the answer travels in the three families `--counter-grid-*`, `--counter-grid-next-*` and `--counter-card-*` (the table above). A section sets all three to the counter of its own ground - `grid` and `card` to it, `grid-next` to the counter of that; a column with a ground sets the card family to the counter of its own; a counter column takes its ground from `grid` and hands `grid-next` to the cards in it. A counter card takes its ground from `card`. So a counter card in an alt section is default, and a counter card in a counter column in an alt section is alt again - the same two steps for every nesting of the three levels. `.nino-cover--dim` and `.nino-img-background--dim` set the dark pair but not the counter families, so a counter column or a counter card inside a dimmed cover or picture takes the counter of its section - alt on a plain section - while `.nino-grid--dim` hands its cards the counter of dark. A page that relied on the old flip of an `.nino-article--alt` inside an alt, tint or dark section writes `.nino-article--counter` now.
- **A picture behind a column.** A column that starts with `<div class="nino-grid-bg"><img ...></div>` has the picture over the whole of it, cut at the corners of the column, and its content above: `.nino-section-bg` for a column. `.nino-grid--dim` darkens it with `--color-background-dim`, as `.nino-cover--dim` does, and is the dark ground whatever else the column names or sits on: it writes in the ink of that ground, hands its roles to what stands in it and the counter of dark, black, to the cards in it, and paints the pair under the picture. A column that says `--dim` with `--alt`, `--tint`, `--primary` or `--counter` is the dark ground all the same.
- **Cards from a loop.** `[stack]` and `[filter]` take the attribute `article`, labelled *Cards* in a builder. Empty, a cell is the `<div>` it was; `card`, `counter`, `alt`, `tint`, `dark`, `black`, `primary` or `brand-alt` make it an `<article class="nino-article ...">` with the modifier of that ground, and `card` with none.
